## Precise additional arithmetic, rather than a format-name distinction

For a normal derived F32 scale, its significand is restricted to **3D=A*2^q+epsilon**, A in [128,255], q in {17,18}, epsilon in {-1,0,1}. Combining this invariant with the existing carry test j*2^J<=D rules out every possible increment. The contradiction ultimately reduces the exceptional q=17 branch to 3jA^2=epsilon (mod 32768), which has no solution under the resulting A/j bounds. The other 100,576 subnormal/zero pairs are completely checked, not assigned this normal-scale invariant.

This is a concrete condition yielding deletion of a computation, rather than “we also use BF16” or a new name for scalar replacement. The generic arbitrary-F32 counterexample shows that deleting the correction without some domain restriction is wrong. It does not prove maximality or novelty of the sufficient restriction.

The closest objection is strong: existing correctly rounded division and double-rounding theory may imply the same fact with a standard specialization. R additionally inspected the author draft [Rump, Ru13b.pdf](https://www.tuhh.de/ti3/paper/rump/Ru13b.pdf), especially Section 4 / Lemma 4.6 and directed-rounding discussion in Section 5. The inspected PDF has draft metadata and is not asserted to be a final published version. Its same-precision input/final-RNE settings do not directly coincide with R4's correlated 8-bit numerator, 24-bit derived divisor and 32-random-bit threshold floor. That distinction requires an explicit theorem-to-theorem mapping before a publication-priority claim; absence of a directly copied formula is insufficient.

## Why “FP4 has only a few bits” is insufficient

R4 output uses an E2M1 grid, but compatibility also depends on32 random comparison bits. Applying a low target-significand double-rounding bound to “FP4 precision” alone ignores that threshold. The generic arbitrary-F32 witness still changes a threshold by1 after RN64. The new certificate is conditional on its actual input domain, not a universal low-precision-format theorem.

Nor does the new result establish a maximal domain or prove both BF16 precision and derived-scale correlation necessary. No unsupported inference is made that every other scale policy fails. A search that finds no identical statement is bounded non-discovery, not worldwide firstness.
