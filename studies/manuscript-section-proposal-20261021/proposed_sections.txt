[P1] Add to the end of Section II: analytical scope

We retain the executable b32, RN64 reference contract above. To characterize
its arithmetic margin, we separately consider virtual binary round-to-nearest,
ties-to-even with p significant bits, denoted R_p, for integer 35<=p<=64 and
the binary64 exponent range. This family is defined for analysis; native GPU
kernels with p=45 or p=49 are outside this extension. Its domain consists of
finite nonnegative BF16
magnitudes 0<=x<=a, where a is the BF16 group maximum and s=RN32(a/6)
uses ties-to-even and gradual underflow. The zero group retains the fallback
2^-42. Sign, Philox addressing, prefix policy, packing and consumer semantics
are unchanged. Unrelated F32 scales, FTZ, NaNs/infinities and arbitrary F32
inputs are outside this domain.

For the E2M1 magnitude grid G=(0,1/2,1,3/2,2,3,4,6), define C(y), 0<=y<6,
as the canonical interval index k together with
floor(2^32*(y-G[k])/(G[k+1]-G[k])), where G[k]<=y<G[k+1]. Set C(6)=(6,2^32).
Let C_p(a,x)=C(min(R_p(x/s),6)) and C_exact(a,x)=C(min(x/s,6)). Write
P_N(p) for C_p=C_exact on all positive normal derived scales, and P_B(p)
for equality on the full stated domain, including positive subnormal scales
and the zero case. These predicates concern canonical thresholds and bases,
not exact real-probability unbiasedness or arbitrary quantizer algorithms.

[P2] Replace the crossing-criterion paragraph in Section III.1

For a nonsaturated ratio r=x/s, let S=33,32,31 for interval widths 1/2,1,2.
Dyadic interpolation separates a common integer endpoint offset. If
r*2^S<1/2, monotonic nearest rounding fixes the half-threshold value and
keeps the result below the first threshold; both thresholds are zero.
Handle this tiny-ratio case before using r's exponent for midpoint spacing.
Exact threshold inputs require no correction. For a non-boundary input on
the remaining path, the next threshold has the same binade immediately to
its left as r, including an upper power-of-two endpoint. In the stated precision
range, it is exactly representable with an even significand.

Write r*2^S=N/D with integer N,D>0 on the remaining path. For a positive remainder define
j=D-(N mod D), Q=ceil(N/D) and E=floor(log2(r)); thus N+j=QD. Upward
threshold crossing occurs exactly when

  j*2^J<=D,  J=p-E-S.                                      (Draft A)

Equality selects the even boundary. Lower thresholds are fixed by monotonic
nearest rounding, and crossing an FP4 endpoint would also cross its threshold
lattice. Exact dyadic interpolation therefore preserves the canonical base
when crossings are excluded. These are standard separation and rounding
arguments. At p53, Draft A specializes to the compatibility test used by B;
its exclusion still requires the actual coupled operand domain.

[P4] Insert Section III.4 after the existing complete-certificate discussion

3.4 Sharp boundaries in the virtual quotient-rounding family

Proposition 2 (computer-assisted precision boundaries). Under the contract
of Section II, for integer 35<=p<=64,

  P_N(p) holds if and only if p>=45;
  P_B(p) holds if and only if p>=49.

These are uniform precision minima for the specified R_p quotient followed
by the fixed b32 threshold construction. They are not precision lower bounds
for every exact FP4 algorithm, and no assertion is made for p<35.

Sufficiency combines analytic reduction with complete small finite
certificates. For normal scales, write a=A*2^(ea-7), 128<=A<=255. The
derived normal F32 significand satisfies the inherited relation
3D=A*2^q+epsilon, where epsilon is in {-1,0,1}, q=18 for A<192 and q=17
otherwise. Normalizing x gives 128<=X<=255 and the exact binade indicator
eta=1[X*2^16<D]. At p45, a hypothetical crossing has L+J=61+eta and a
positive integer t satisfying

  t<(A^2+2^(46-2q))*2^-J.                                  (Draft B)

The resulting normal certificate covers 1600 coefficient/t branches and 2814
retained J/eta branches. Its 236 residue solutions all fail: 234 reconstruct
X outside 128..255, and two contradict their assumed eta. The checker
accounts for every member of each candidate residue class without trusting
the generator's modular-inverse routine. This replaces the old normal
representative-set certificate as a necessary dependency of this proof;
the earlier result itself is retained.

The subnormal p49 argument uses D=s*2^149 and distinct q=15,16,17 classes.
Its complete 4656-branch certificate leaves one relaxed congruence solution,
which has no legal BF16 reconstruction. A separately constructed direct-j
implementation ran once and agreed on every branch and all six
reconstructions, after 124640 direct congruence checks. The auditor's prior
knowledge of the earlier conclusion was disclosed: this is implementation
independence, not a blind replication or proof-assistant verification.
Normal p45 sufficiency, subnormal p49 sufficiency, zero/saturation handling
and precision monotonicity give the full p49 statement. The proposed
Appendix A identifies these separate proof dependencies and their hashes.

Necessity follows from legal counterexamples in the same domain. The normal
pair with BF16 magnitude codes a=0x01ec,x=0x00c7 fails at p44: its ideal
and rounded thresholds are 2274877096 and 2274877097. The subnormal pair
a=0x00f2,x=0x00dd fails at p48, with thresholds 3176857458 and 3176857459.
The preserved exact identities and crossing criterion extend the failures
to p35..44 and p35..48 respectively. Their raw-code lexicographic
minimality is a separate inherited ordering result; a single legal failure,
not that ordering result, supplies each precision lower bound.

The original correction-removal result at p53 was already established by
the normal argument and complete CPU evidence. The new result characterizes
its precision margin and supplies smaller proof dependencies; it is not a
rediscovery of p53 correctness or a new native performance measurement.

[P5] Replace the first two paragraphs of Section VI

Our rounding tools are established: exact dyadic interpolation, separation
from representable thresholds, monotonicity and integer quotient/remainder
analysis. Roux's Theorem 29 [5] assumes an even radix, operands in FLX_p1,
nonzero denominator, two nearest modes and p2>=2*p1. Admitting the present
operands to FLX24 gives the direct nearest24 double-rounding identity for
p2>=48, including 49 and 53. That conclusion is not the fixed b32 threshold
floor; the direct p1=24 substitution also does not admit p2=45. This
statement concerns the named theorem's direct use, not non-derivability
from Roux's entire framework or other mixed-format results.

Saiki et al.'s obtained v1 text [7, Section 5.2, Figure 8] supplies generic safe
format-containment rules for the displayed equal-directed and round-to-odd
mode chains, with their padding and non-overflow premises. Our positive
scaled probability has an RNE intermediate followed by a floor/RTZ target;
that unrestricted chain is not one of those generic safe rules. Replacing
legacy RN64 with round-to-odd would require a separate equivalence argument.
Their semantics-preserving rewrite methodology is prior work, not a new
framework introduced here. Additional operand invariants may be expressible
within such frameworks.

Rump's article [6] is relevant prior work, but a premise-level comparison
with its exact Lemma 4.6 remains unresolved. The exact body, assumptions,
conclusion and author-draft/final-version correspondence are not currently
verified. Earlier reading notes report inspection of an author draft and
pinned Lean source; those historical attestations are not a fresh check of
the precise lemma bodies in the present artifact. No Lean/Coq execution is
claimed. We do not infer novelty from unavailable text or from one failed
direct theorem substitution.

The contract-specific work here is the joint BF16 coefficient, RN32(a/6)
divisor, b32 lattice and eta-binade exclusion, together with complete finite
certificates and explicit failure witnesses. Its derivability from all
closest theory and publication priority remain open. The remaining SR,
FP4-selection and GPU implementation antecedents discussed below remain
relevant; differing scale or random-word contracts alone do not establish
an original algorithm or a universally superior implementation.

[P6] Replace the limitations paragraphs of Section VII, before its conclusion

The p=45 and p=49 statements have a fixed finite-input and virtual-precision scope.
Their sufficient clauses use analytic reductions and complete finite
certificates. A short symbolic proof replacing the coefficient exclusions,
and a proof-assistant development, remain open. The normal p53 analytic
argument and the sharper computer-assisted boundaries have different proof
forms. The subnormal independent implementation was not blind and shares
the written mathematical specification. The full-domain theorem is a
composition of normal, subnormal and zero/saturation obligations; it is
neither a mechanically checked CUDA proof nor a maximal-domain theorem.

No native p45/p49 kernel was implemented or measured by this analytical
extension. The historical CUDA comparisons retain their original scope:
separate H100 SXM and NVL sessions reported producer-time reductions over
corrected B of 3.15% and 3.65%. Strong scalar FP64 A-double-prime remains
faster on the ordinary/model-derived and Gaussian conditions; on the NVL
core producer comparison, D1 takes 16.81% longer. Repeated synthetic edge
tensors are a separate fixed condition. The precision theorem adds no
speedup measurement and does not alter those unfavorable comparisons.
Temporal-cycle intervals characterize the fixed process/device sessions,
not repeated rentals or workload families. No new mechanism, achieved
occupancy, or pure FP64/local-access contribution is established.

Bit-exact finite-word threshold compatibility is not exact real-probability
unbiasedness, statistical independence of a random stream, or restored BF16
model quality. BF16 reconstruction, residual addition and application
behavior require their own evidence. End-to-end LLM acceleration and
public-library superiority remain unproved. The older 31.43% result used
another scale/tuning contract and is not combined with these gains.

Exact Rump Lemma 4.6 premises, conclusion and version correspondence remain
unverified, as do current code-level checks of the historically cited Lean
lemma bodies. The exact historical final novelty/limitations report has not
been recovered and is not replaced by a neighboring review. These source
gaps cannot be counted as evidence of originality. The paper claims no new
general rounding framework, worldwide firstness or formal verification.
