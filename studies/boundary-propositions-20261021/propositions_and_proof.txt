Precision-boundary propositions and proof obligations
2026-10-21

Status

The propositions below are certificate-assisted mathematical statements about
a fixed finite input domain. Their positive universal clauses use the sealed
round-1 finite certificates plus the written reductions given here. The new
run independently establishes the small failure witnesses and their specified
lexicographic minimality. This is not a proof-assistant development, a new
generic double-rounding theorem, or a performance result.

Definitions

B is the set of nonnegative finite BF16 magnitudes, with their monotonically
ordered unsigned magnitude codes 0..32639. D contains pairs (a,x) in B x B
with x<=a. Such pairs are realizable by a group containing a, x and zeros.
For a>0, let s(a)=RN32(a/6), with ties to even and gradual underflow. At a=0
use the unchanged fallback 2^-42. No independent or arbitrary F32 scale is
admitted. D_N is the subset with a>0 and normal positive F32 s(a). D_S has positive
subnormal s(a). The zero group is handled separately.

Let G=(0,1/2,1,3/2,2,3,4,6). For y in [0,6), define the unique k in 0..6
such that G[k]<=y<G[k+1], and

 C_b(y) = (k, floor(2^b * (y-G[k])/(G[k+1]-G[k]))).

Set C_b(6)=(6,2^b). This is the canonical base and integer threshold, not
merely equality of output distributions. For r=x/s(a), define

 C_exact(a,x,b) = C_b(min(r,6)),
 C_rounded(a,x,b,p) = C_b(min(R_p(r),6)),

where R_p is binary round-to-nearest-even with p significand bits and the
binary64 exponent range. In this study 35<=p<=64. On this domain the ratio
and all relevant intermediate dyadic interpolation values avoid that range's
underflow/overflow. FTZ is not enabled. This is virtual arithmetic, not an
assertion that a native p45 or p49 GPU instruction exists.

P_E(b,p) means equality of these two canonical pairs for every (a,x) in E.
The original paper's arithmetic setting is P_D(32,53). Values b>32 are
mathematical probability lattices; no Philox word-address extension is supplied.

Proposition 1: normal-scale precision boundary

For integer p in [35,64],

 P_D_N(32,p) holds if and only if p>=45.

Sufficiency is certificate-assisted: the inherited direct validator has zero
canonical mismatches at p45 on 630848 normal representative pairs. Lemmas A-C
below extend those classes to D_N, and precision monotonicity extends p45 to
the rest of the stated interval. Necessity follows from the legal p44 witness
below, the same monotonicity lemma, and the new direct evaluations at every
p=35..44. No minimum for p<35 is asserted.

Proposition 2: full-domain precision boundary

For integer p in [35,64],

 P_D(32,p) holds if and only if p>=49.

The normal part follows from Proposition 1. The inherited direct p49 subnormal
certificate covers all 100575 pairs with positive a codes 1..447 and x<=a.
Zero is exact. The p48 witness below establishes necessity throughout 35..48;
the new bit-serial reference explicitly evaluates those precisions. Thus 49
is a necessary and sufficient uniform precision threshold in the stated p
interval for this domain, not for arbitrary F32 inputs or scale policies.

Proposition 3: probability-lattice boundary at fixed p53

For integer b in [1,40],

 P_D(b,53) holds if and only if b<=37.

The positive b37 clause uses the inherited direct certificate: 712768 normal
representative pairs and all 100575 subnormal pairs pass. Zero is exact.
For a fixed interval, flooring a finer lattice and then coarsening gives the
same integer as direct coarsening, so canonical equality at b37 implies it
at every smaller b. The fixed b38 witness has the same base and a strictly
larger rounded probability. A difference of at least one lattice unit cannot
vanish on any finer lattice. It therefore refutes every b in 38..40. This is
not an application-level claim about changing the original 32-bit RNG policy.

Standard proof units

Lemma A: exact interpolation and the upward-crossing criterion.

For an exact nonsaturated ratio r in one interval, let its width be Delta,
S=b-log2(Delta), E=floor(log2(r)), and write r=n/d with integers n>=0,d>0.
Subtracting the lower endpoint and dividing by the dyadic interval width is
exact after rounding r: nonzero intervals satisfy Sterbenz's ratio condition,
the zero endpoint is direct, and dyadic scaling is exact. An integer offset
therefore separates the desired threshold from floor(r*2^S).

For b32 and p>=35, and for b37/b38 at p53, the relevant threshold boundaries
are representable with even least significand bit. Their scaled absolute
numerators need at most b+2 bits; p>=b+3 supplies the zero low bit. Monotonicity
of R_p, fixing each boundary, excludes downward floor loss. A nonzero remainder
has next-boundary distance

 rem = (n*2^S) mod d,
 j = d-rem,
 distance = j/(d*2^S).

The half spacing below that boundary is 2^(E-p), including an upper power-of-two
boundary. Nearest-even reaches the boundary exactly when

 j * 2^(p-E-S) <= d.                                      (A1)

Equality chooses the even boundary. If rem=0, the ratio is already a lattice
boundary and no correction is needed. Equation A1 is standard remainder and
half-ULP reasoning. It is not the new contribution. For fixed b it also proves
that absence of a crossing at p0 implies absence for every p>=p0 in the stated
representability region. Crossing an FP4 endpoint would cross one of these
boundaries; therefore the canonical base is preserved when no crossing occurs.

Lemma B: normal-scale exponent reduction and the small-ratio tail.

Write a=A*2^(ea-7), x=X*2^(ex-7), with A,X in 128..255. Normalized BF16
subnormals have trailing zeros in the integer significand, so they are included.
For a normal derived F32 scale, power-of-two rescaling commutes with its RNE
scale generation without changing the rounded significand. Thus its ratio is
represented by a'=A/128, x'=X*2^(t-7), t=ex-ea<=0, with X<=A when t=0.
The representative scale exponent is -3 for A<192 and -2 otherwise.

For each b, the sealed certificate includes every A,X and t in [-(b+6),0]
subject to that legality condition. These representatives are themselves valid
BF16 values. The class count is 16384*(b+6)+8256: 630848 at b32 and 712768
at b37. Zero inputs are exact separately.

For t<-(b+6), the representative scale is at least RN32(1/6)>1/6. Hence
r<12*2^t<3*2^(-b-4). Even after RNE at the stated precisions it remains below
the first threshold point 2^(-b-1). Both thresholds are zero. All nonzero
ratios of the full BF16/derived-scale domain exceed 2^-259 and are below 8;
p<=64 stays well within binary64's exponent range. This tail does not discard
an intermediate underflow case or turn arbitrary F32 data into BF16 data.

Lemma C: subnormal-scale classification.

At BF16 code a=448, a/6 is exactly 2^-126, the smallest normal F32 value.
Monotonicity and the positive minimum scale show that codes 1..447 produce
positive subnormal F32 scales. Every x code 0..a is explicitly checked there:
sum(a+1,a=1..447)=100575. The all-zero group adds one separate exact case.
The normal-scale identity is not reused unchanged for subnormal scales.

New independent witnesses and minimality

bits followed by guard/sticky and ties-even. It does not use A1 to choose an
answer. F32 scales use exact encoding bisection and an independent midpoint
check. All 340 scales agree. Integer nearest-neighbor checks: 195. Prefix
evaluations: 78552 total. Witness precision checks: 84. Zero checks: 3.

An analytic explanation of W3

Here a=2^-129, x=2^-131, and in F32 subnormal units

 D=174763=(2^19+1)/3,
 s=D*2^-149,
 r=2^18/D.

At b38 the interval is [1,1.5], S=39. As 2^19 == -1 (mod D),
2^57 == -1 (mod D). The next boundary is

 q/2^39, q=(2^57+1)/D=824632147971,

and its distance above r is 1/(D*2^39). Since E=0 and
2^(53-39)=16384<=D, it is within the binary64 half-ULP. The boundary is
exactly representable and even at p53, so R_53(r)=q/2^39. Subtracting the
lower endpoint yields the one-unit threshold discrepancy in W3.

At b37 the same rounded ratio has scaled value q/2, with q odd. The exact
scaled value is q/2-1/(2D), so both floors equal (q-1)/2. Both b37 thresholds
for this pair are 137438167041. This explains this witness's sharp transition;
it does not by itself prove the universal safe b37 clause, which still uses F3/F4.

Existing theory versus the additional domain result

Exact dyadic interpolation, A1, precision monotonicity, exponent normalization,
and finite checking are established numerical reasoning. The historical
normal-scale relation 3D=A*2^q+epsilon, q in {17,18}, epsilon in {-1,0,1},
is the restricted divisor information used by the original paper's p53 proof.
The new boundary propositions characterize this same coupled domain more
sharply; their numerical boundary values have not been derived here from a new
general symbolic theorem. They are not claims that each domain assumption is
necessary, that this domain is maximal, or that BF16 alone suffices.

Roux Theorem 29 directly gives a nearest-rounding result when both operands
and the target belong to its stated same-precision format. Setting p1=24,
p2=53 does not replace the fixed b32 floor by that target. Saiki v1's generic
safe mode rules do not include unrestricted RNE->RTZ. These direct mapping
failures leave an operand-set separation obligation; they do not establish
independence from the authors' entire frameworks or Flocq. Rump's exact Lemma
4.6 remains OPEN after official university access timed out and publisher
access returned 403. No certificate or authorization check was bypassed.

The exact missing historical final-limits report also remains unidentified.
No neighboring contribution report is substituted. No new GPU implementation,
native timing, model-quality result, or superiority over A-double-prime is claimed.
