Analytic denominator reduction and a conservative complete no-carry proof
2026-10-21

Outcome and scope

This document does NOT close the sharp symbolic p45/p49 sufficiency proof.
It proves a common divisor/residue reduction, gives analytic precision lower
bounds from the known witnesses, and supplies a complete elementary argument
for b32,p53 on the derived-subnormal-scale domain. Combined with the existing
normal-scale argument, this gives a conservative full-domain p53 sufficient
condition without the historical 100575-pair subnormal certificate.

The elementary argument uses ten displayed constant integer brackets and five
displayed nonsquares. These are explicit arithmetic cases in the proof, not a
hidden input sweep or a large finite certificate. The companion checker only
recomputes those constants and a few fixed examples. This is a proof draft,
not proof-assistant verification or a publication-novelty claim.

1. Fixed predicate

Inputs are nonnegative finite BF16 magnitudes 0<=x<=a. For a>0,
s=RN32(a/6), ties-to-even, gradual underflow. At a=0 use 2^-42.
The grid is (0,1/2,1,3/2,2,3,4,6), b=32, and virtual RNE-p has the
binary64 exponent range, 35<=p<=64. No arbitrary F32 inputs/scales or FTZ
change is admitted. The finite ratio has no working underflow/overflow here.

Indeed the smallest positive a/6 is (2^15/3) F32-subnormal units, so its
relative subnormal rounding error is less than 1/20000. Normal-scale relative
rounding error is at most 2^-24. Thus s>a/8 and r<8. Also s<2^126 while
nonzero x>=2^-133, giving r>2^-259. For p<=64 the smallest needed working
quantum is at least 2^-322, well inside the binary64 exponent range.

For y in [0,6), select the unique interval [l,h) from that grid and define
C(y)=(canonical interval index, floor(2^32*(y-l)/(h-l))). At y=6 use
the last interval index and threshold 2^32. Let P_E(p) assert

 C(min(RNE_p(x/s),6)) = C(min(x/s,6)) for every pair in E.

E_N has a>0 and normal positive F32 s; E_S has subnormal positive F32 s;
E is their union with the all-zero pair. Sign and packing are unchanged
composition outside this scalar predicate. This is not a proof of CUDA
execution or of altered-precision producer binaries.

2. Standard crossing lemma

On a nonsaturated exact interval, S=33,32,31 for widths 1/2,1,2.
Let r=x/s, E=floor(log2(r)), N/D=r*2^S with integer N,D>0.
For rem=N mod D nonzero, set j=D-rem and Q=ceil(N/D), so N+j=QD.
The next threshold boundary is j/(D*2^S) above r. Its binary midpoint
distance is 2^(E-p). The boundaries are even representable values when
p>=35. Thus an upward threshold crossing is equivalent to

 j*2^J <= D,  J=p-E-S.                                    (1)

There is no downward floor loss because the lower threshold boundary is
representable and nearest rounding is monotone. Subtraction of l is exact
by Sterbenz on each nonzero interval; the zero endpoint and dyadic scaling
are exact directly. Excluding all threshold crossings also excludes crossing
an FP4 endpoint, hence preserves the canonical base. Zero and saturation
are immediate cases. These are established rounding/separation arguments.

For b32, E+S<=33 and Q<=2^34. At p53, J>=20. A positive remainder with
D<2^24 also forces J<=23. We use those format and lattice facts below.

3. Scale structure, with different units kept explicit

Normal scales. Normalize a=A*2^(ea-7), 128<=A<=255. Write the normal F32
scale with its 24-bit significand D. Its rounded division by six gives

 3D=A*2^q+epsilon, epsilon in {-1,0,1},
 q=18 for A<192, q=17 for A>=192.                          (2)

This is the historical restricted-divisor identity. For normalized BF16 x
with significand X, its scaled quotient numerator is N=X*2^L, and
L+J=p+16+eta, eta in {0,1}. If L<0, r*2^S<2^-16; RNE at p>=35 cannot
reach the first threshold boundary and both floors are zero. Otherwise N is
an integer and (1) applies with this D. At p53 a carry forces J<=23 and
therefore L>=46.

Subnormal scales. Use the UNNORMALIZED scale integer D=s*2^149. Here
the F32 spacing is exactly 2^-149, not the normal 24-bit-significand spacing.
Positive a codes 1..447 have these exact classes:

 a code             A range       q     a in BF16-minimum units 2^-133
 1..255             1..255       15     A
 256..383           128..255     16     2A
 384..447           128..191     17     4A

In these units a/6 = (A*2^q/3)*2^-149. Consequently the same-shaped identity

 3D=A*2^q+epsilon, epsilon in {-1,0,1}                       (3)

holds, now for a different integer D and q15/16/17. There is no halfway
case: the only noninteger fractions have denominator three. In particular
epsilon=0 exactly when A is divisible by three. At a code 448 the scale
is exactly 2^-126; this separates all positive subnormal scales.

Write nonzero x=X*2^v*2^-133, with 1<=X<=255 and v in {0,1,2}, restricted
by x<=a. Then r=X*2^(16+v)/D, N=X*2^L and L=16+v+S>=47. D<2^23.
Equations (2) and (3) must not be confused: normalizing the subnormal D to
24 bits would multiply epsilon by a power of two. No such substitution
is made in this proof.

4. Common residue lemma

Assume N=X*2^L, L>=2q, epsilon=+1 or -1, and a carry candidate (1).
Then D is odd and gcd(A,D)=1. Since A*2^q == -epsilon (mod D),

 A^2*N == X*2^(L-2q) (mod D).

Putting u=L-2q, the positive integer

 t=(j*A^2+X*2^u)/D                                        (4)

exists. Conversely, if (4) is an integer and 0<j<D, then gcd(A,D)=1
implies D divides N+j, so this is the same complementary-remainder condition.
Two useful exact identities and a bound follow:

 3X*2^u = t*(A*2^q+epsilon)-3j*A^2,                       (5)
 Q = t*2^(2q)-3j*(A*2^q-epsilon),                         (6)
 0<t < 2^(34-2q)+A^2*2^(-J).                             (7)

For (7), substitute X*2^u=(QD-j)/2^(2q) into (4), obtaining
t=Q/2^(2q)+j*A^2/D-j/(D*2^(2q)), then use (1) and Q<=2^34.
The strict inequality is useful when bounding the integer t.

Equation (5) gives the safe congruence

 3j*A^2 == epsilon*t (mod 2^m), m=min(u,q).                (8)

The exponent min(u,q) is essential. For subnormal q15, u is at least 17,
so reducing to modulus 2^u does NOT eliminate the t*A*2^q term. Copying
the normal-scale congruence with that larger modulus would be an invalid
shortcut. The following proof uses only valid fixed moduli.

If epsilon=0, D is divisible by 2^q and N is also divisible by 2^q. Thus
j must be a positive multiple of 2^q. At p53, (1) gives j<=15 in the
normal case and j<=7 in the subnormal case. Since q>=15 this is impossible.
Only epsilon=+/-1 remains.

5. Complete subnormal-scale sufficient condition at p53

Theorem S53. Under the fixed contract, P_E_S(53) holds.

At p53, J>=20. Bounds from (3) and (7), plus the possible L, give:

 q    A range      j upper bound    t possibilities    u lower bound
 15   1..255              2             1..16               17
 16   128..255            5             1..4                15
 17   128..191            7             1                   13

For example q15 has D<3*2^20 and t<16+255^2/2^20<17. The other rows
follow in the same way: t<4+255^2/2^20<5 and t<1+191^2/2^20<2.

5.1 q15

Use (8) modulo 2^15: 3j*A^2=epsilon*t+k*32768 with 1<=t<=16.
If j=1, (1) requires D>=2^20 and hence A>=96. Then 1<=k<=5.
If j=2, (1) requires D>=2^21 and hence A>=192. Then 7<=k<=11.

For each possible k the adjacent integer square brackets below leave a
distance greater than 16 from 3j*A^2 to k*32768. Monotonicity of A^2 for
positive A excludes every other integer A as well. The table shows signed
residuals at the two adjacent A values, not a scan of BF16 input pairs.

 j   k    lower A   upper A    lower residual   upper residual
 1   1      104       105          -320              307
 1   2      147       148          -709              176
 1   3      181       182           -21             1068
 1   4      209       210           -29             1228
 1   5      233       234          -973              428
 2   7      195       196         -1226             1120
 2   8      209       210           -58             2456
 2   9      221       222         -1866              792
 2  10      233       234         -1946              856
 2  11      245       246          -298             2648

None can equal epsilon*t, whose absolute value is at most 16. This is a
complete contradiction for q15.

5.2 q16

Use (8) modulo 2^15, with 1<=j<=5 and 1<=t<=4.

If A is even, t must be divisible by four, so t=4. If A is divisible by
four, reduction modulo 16 is already contradictory. Otherwise A=2B with
B odd, 64<=B<=127. Divide by four:

 3j*B^2 == epsilon (mod 8192).

Since odd squares are one modulo eight, only j=3,epsilon=+1 or
j=5,epsilon=-1 remain. For j3, (3B-1)(3B+1) must be divisible by 8192.
The two positive even factors have gcd two; one would have to be at least
4096, but both are at most 382. For j5, 15B^2+1=m*8192, 8<=m<=29.
Modulo 15 forces m=8 or 23, giving B^2=4369 or 12561. Neither is a square:
66^2<4369<67^2 and 112^2<12561<113^2.

If A is odd, reduction modulo eight leaves exactly these cases:

 (j,t,epsilon)=(1,3,+1),(2,2,-1),(3,1,+1),(4,4,+1),
                (4,4,-1),(5,1,-1).

The first requires A^2==1 (mod 32768); factors A-1,A+1 are too small,
by the same gcd-two argument. The second requires 3A^2==-1 (mod 16384),
contradicting modulo eight. The third requires (3A)^2==1 (mod 32768),
whose factors are at most 766, again too small. Both j4 cases require
3A^2==+/-1 (mod 8192), also impossible modulo eight. Finally j5 requires
15A^2+1=m*32768, 8<=m<=29. Modulo 15 leaves m=17, so A^2=37137,
strictly between 192^2 and 193^2. Thus q16 is excluded.

5.3 q17

Here t=1, j<=7, and (8) holds modulo 8192. A and j must be odd. Modulo
eight leaves j3,epsilon=+1 or j5,epsilon=-1. The j3 factors 3A-1,3A+1
are at most 574, so the gcd-two argument again excludes divisibility by
8192. For j5, 15A^2+1=m*8192 with 30<=m<=67. Modulo 15 leaves only
m=38 or 53. These give A^2=20753 or 28945, lying strictly between
144^2 and 145^2, or 170^2 and 171^2, respectively. Both are impossible.

Every possible subnormal branch has now been excluded. This proves S53
without the old 100575-pair enumeration. The thirteen scale examples in
verification.json are sanity checks of units and encoding; they are NOT
the coverage argument. Coverage comes from the classes and the case proof.

6. Normal p53 and the conservative full-domain consequence

The historical normal-scale proof can also be expressed through this lemma.
For q18, A<=191 and (7) gives t<1/4+191^2/2^20<1, impossible. For q17,
A<=255, (7) gives t<1+255^2/2^20<2, so t=1, while j<=10.
Equation (6) gives Q>2^33; hence E+S=33 and J=20. From L+J=69+eta,
u=L-34 is 15 or 16. Reduce (8) modulo 32768. Parity and modulo eight
leave j3,epsilon=+1 or j5,epsilon=-1. The j3 factor bound is at most
766<16384. The j5 case again forces A^2=37137. Both fail, as in the
existing normal argument. This part is inherited reasoning, not a new result.

Together with S53 and exact zero/saturation cases, the full scalar predicate
P_E(53) follows analytically. By the standard precision monotonicity lemma,
P_E(p) also holds for every 53<=p<=64. This is a conservative sufficient
bound, NOT a proof that the true minimum is 53. The known p48 witness gives
an analytic lower bound of 49; the exact value 49 still uses the earlier
finite sufficiency certificate.

7. Analytic necessity at the sharp lower boundaries

The following identities give mathematical precision lower bounds. Their
lexicographic raw-code minimality, checked in the previous round, is a separate
statement and is not needed for these lower bounds.

Normal witness: a code 0x01ec, x code 0x00c7. Here A=236,q17,epsilon=-1,
D=10310997,X=199,S=33,E=0,L=49,j=2845. Equations (4)-(6) give t=16.
At p44, j*2^11=5826560<=D, but at p45 j*2^12=11653120>D.
The exact identity is 199*2^49+2845=10864811689*10310997.
Thus this legal pair fails for every p35..44 and establishes that a uniform
normal-scale precision in the stated interval must be at least 45. Its
thresholds at p44 are 2274877096 and 2274877097. The pair's passing at p45
does not prove uniform sufficiency at p45.

Subnormal witness: a code 0x00f2, x code 0x00dd. Here A=242,q15,
epsilon=-1,D=2643285,X=221,S=31,E=2,L=47,j=47,t=12.
At p48, j*2^15=1540096<=D, but at p49 j*2^16=3080192>D.
The exact identity is 221*2^47+47=11766792051*2643285.
It fails for every p35..48 and establishes the full-domain uniform lower
bound 49. Its p48 thresholds are 3176857458 and 3176857459. Again the
pair passing p49 is not the universal sufficient clause.

8. Exact remaining sharp-sufficiency obligations

R45, normal scales. A carry at p45 would have 12<=J<=23,
L=61+eta-J>=38, u=L-2q, epsilon=+/-1 and (4)-(5). Bound (7) gives
t<=9 for q18,A128..191, or t<=16 for q17,A192..255. X is in 128..255.
The epsilon=0 branch is already impossible: j would be a positive multiple
of 2^q but (1) bounds it by 4095.
The unresolved exclusion must retain x<=a, eta=1[X*2^16<D], the exact
ratio exponent E=45-J-S, and the selected interval width defining S.
No proof is supplied that these small-t Diophantine conditions have no legal
solution. The old finite p45 certificate is still needed for sharp sufficiency.

R49, subnormal scales. Keep the three classes of (3). For q15, x has v0
and 1<=X<=A. For q16, x has v0,X1..255 or v1,X128..A. For q17, x has
v0,X1..255, v1,X128..255, or v2,X128..A. Put L=16+v+S, u=L-2q,
J=49-E-S>=16. Bounds give t<=16,4,1 for q15,16,17, respectively, but
j can now be as large as 42,85,127. The small-j case exclusions of S53
therefore do not close R49. The full equation (5) and valid modulus (8)
must be kept; the ten constant brackets above do not cover this larger set.
The epsilon=0 branch is excluded by j<=127<2^q and divisibility.
Sharp full-domain p49 sufficiency remains certificate-assisted, not analytic.

9. Interpretation

New: the unnormalized subnormal divisor identity, the common t reduction,
the complete conservative S53 proof draft, and explicit analytic necessity
calculations. Inherited: exact p45/p49 finite safety certificates, raw-code
minimality, and the existing normal p53 argument. Still open: R45/R49,
proof-assistant checking, complete closest-theory derivability, and publication
novelty. Known rounding and integer arguments remain the mathematical tools;
this is not a new generic SR algorithm, hardware claim or faster quantizer.
