1. BF16 encoding and subnormal-scale classification

Let e and f be the unsigned BF16 exponent and fraction fields. In units
delta=2^-133, a BF16 magnitude is f for e=0, and (128+f)*2^(e-1) for e>0.
It follows directly, including the exponent-zero/exponent-one transition,
that raw codes 1..255 are exactly A*delta with A=code in 1..255. Codes
256..383 are 2A*delta with A=code-128 in 128..255. Codes 384..447 are
4A*delta with A=code-256 in 128..191. Thus no representation is lost at
codes 127/128, 255/256, or 383/384.

F32 subnormal spacing is 2^-149, and delta/2^-149=2^16. Dividing each
class by six gives A*2^q/3 F32 subnormal units, with q=15,16,17. Rounding
this rational to the nearest integer gives D with 3D=A*2^q+epsilon,
epsilon in {-1,0,+1}. The possible fractional parts are 0,1/3,2/3, so
there is no tie. epsilon=0 iff 3 divides A, because 2^q is invertible
modulo three.

Code 447 gives D=8344917<2^23; code 448 is 768*delta and its division by
six is exactly 2^-126, or 2^23 subnormal units. Monotonicity of RN32 and
these endpoints show that the positive subnormal-scale codes are exactly
1..447. There is no zero rounded scale: code 1 already gives D=10923.
For these a, any positive x<=a has x=X*2^v*delta, 1<=X<=255, v in {0,1,2},
with the usual physical BF16 restrictions. The S53 proof safely relaxes
those restrictions after obtaining this representation.

Consequently r=X*2^(16+v)/D. For interval scaling S in {31,32,33},
N=X*2^L with L=16+v+S>=47. This is an integer in the unnormalized
subnormal units used throughout the proof. Normalizing D to 24 bits would
change epsilon; the reviewed argument does not make that substitution.

2. Threshold crossing and its hypotheses on this domain

Within a grid interval, its lower endpoint times 2^S is an integer, so the
next threshold is B=ceil(r*2^S)*2^-S. With a nonzero remainder of N/D,
write j=D-(N mod D), Q=ceil(N/D), and N+j=QD. Then B-r=j/(D*2^S).
Exact threshold inputs have zero remainder and are exactly representable.

On E_S, N/D>2^24 follows from L>=47, X>=1, and D<2^23. Thus E+S>=24,
where E=floor(log2 r). Both bounding binade endpoints are on this threshold
lattice, and B has left-hand binade E, including B=2^(E+1). Its distance
to the immediately preceding p-bit value is 2^(E-p+1). Therefore its
lower midpoint is 2^(E-p) below B. The bound E+S<=33 makes B an even
representable value for p>=35, including a possible binade endpoint.
The midpoint tie selects B. This proves the necessary and sufficient
upward carry condition j*2^(p-E-S)<=D on the audited domain, including
the inclusive equality in that condition.

Each exact lower threshold is representable; monotonicity of nearest
rounding therefore prevents a downward floor loss. Grid endpoints are
thresholds too, so a canonical-interval change cannot evade this test.
For r>=6, monotonicity and exact representability of 6 preserve saturation;
zero is exact. These facts cover the zero, exact-threshold, endpoint, and
saturation cases omitted by the positive-remainder argument.

For r<6, the grid gives E+S<=33 and Q<=2^34. At p=53, J=53-E-S>=20.
Hence j<=floor(D/2^20). These are bounds on a hypothetical carry, not a
claim that every interval attains them.

3. The positive-integer t transform

For epsilon=+/-1, D is odd since 3D is even plus or minus one. Also
gcd(A,D)=1: a common divisor would divide 3D-A*2^q=epsilon. The relation
A*2^q=-epsilon modulo D implies A^2*2^(2q)=1 modulo D.
Because L>=47>=2q, put u=L-2q>=0. Multiplying N+j=0 modulo D by A^2
then gives X*2^u+j*A^2=0 modulo D. Its quotient t is a strictly positive
integer. Conversely this divisibility and gcd(A,D)=1 restore D|(N+j).
No sign, integrality, or invertibility hypothesis is missing.

Multiplying tD=j*A^2+X*2^u by three gives equation (5). Substitution of
X*2^u=(QD-j)/2^(2q), followed by
((A*2^q)^2-1)/D=3*(A*2^q-epsilon), gives equation (6). The same
substitution gives
  t=Q/2^(2q)+j*A^2/D-j/(D*2^(2q)).
The last term is strictly positive. Together with Q<=2^34 and
j/D<=2^-J, this proves the strict upper bound (7).

Reducing (5) modulo 2^min(u,q) correctly eliminates both power-of-two
terms and gives (8). Using 2^u instead when u>q would be invalid. The
reviewed q15 argument uses 2^15, and no such invalid strengthening occurs.

For epsilon=0, both D and N are divisible by 2^q. Their complementary
remainder j would then be a positive multiple of 2^q. Since a p53 carry
has j<=7 and q>=15, this branch is impossible.

5. q15: completeness of the ten brackets

The congruence is 3j*A^2=epsilon*t+k*32768, |epsilon*t|<=16.
For j=1, D>=2^20 and 3D=A*32768+epsilon imply A>=96.
Using 96<=A<=255 and the residual bound forces integer 1<=k<=5:
  (3*96^2-16)/32768 <= k <= (3*255^2+16)/32768.
For j=2, D>=2^21 similarly implies A>=192, and
  (6*192^2-16)/32768 <= k <= (6*255^2+16)/32768
forces 7<=k<=11. Thus there are exactly the ten displayed k cases;
neither a k=0 case nor a negative k case has been omitted.

The standalone checker constructed each lower root as
isqrt(floor(k*32768/(3j))) and the upper root as lower+1. It reproduced
all ten displayed brackets and signed residuals. In every case the lower
residual is below -16 and the upper residual above +16. Strict monotonicity
of 3j*A^2 for positive integer A excludes every other A. This proves the
case without inspecting any x or any collection of raw input pairs.

6. q16: all parity cases and reductions

The valid modulus is 2^15. If A is even, four divides t and 1<=t<=4
forces t=4. If four divides A, reduction modulo 16 gives 0=+/-4,
impossible. Otherwise A=2B with B odd in 64..127. Dividing by four
leaves 3j*B^2=epsilon modulo 8192. Odd squares are one modulo eight;
with j=1..5 only (j,epsilon)=(3,+1),(5,-1) survive.

For j3, (3B-1)(3B+1) is divisible by 2^13. Both factors are positive
even integers with gcd two. One therefore has valuation at least 12 and
must be at least 4096, contradicting their upper bound 382.
For j5, 15B^2+1=m*8192, and the safe range 8<=m<=29 with reduction
modulo 15 gives m=8 or 23. The resulting values 4369 and 12561 are
strictly between 66^2/67^2 and 112^2/113^2, respectively.

If A is odd, 3j=epsilon*t modulo eight gives exactly the six cases
listed in the source. The independent 40-combination check confirms this
tiny parity table. Their exclusions are algebraically valid:
  (1,3,+): invert three modulo 32768 to get A^2=1;
  (2,2,-): divide by two to get 3A^2=-1 modulo 16384;
  (3,1,+): get (3A)^2=1 modulo 32768;
  (4,4,+/-): divide by four to get 3A^2=+/-1 modulo 8192;
  (5,1,-): get 15A^2+1=m*32768.
The j2 and j4 equations fail modulo eight. The j1 and j3 products have
positive even gcd-two factors and would need a factor at least 16384;
their maxima are 256 and 766. The j5 range 8..29 leaves m=17 modulo
15, giving 37137, strictly between 192^2 and 193^2. All are excluded.

7. q17: all remaining cases

Here t=1 and the valid modulus is 8192. Parity forces A and j odd.
Modulo eight with j<=7 leaves precisely (j,epsilon)=(3,+1),(5,-1).
For j3 the positive gcd-two factors 3A-1 and 3A+1 would require a
factor at least 4096, contradicting the upper bound 574.
For j5, 15A^2+1=m*8192. The loose published range 30..67 is safe
(the tighter integer range is 31..66). Reduction modulo 15 leaves
m=38 or 53. Their values 20753 and 28945 lie strictly between
144^2/145^2 and 170^2/171^2. Both fail. The loose endpoints do not
omit any case or create a false exclusion.

8. Limited executable checks and limits

The successful independent run checked 21 fixed scale codes near encoding
and proof boundaries, and 143 explicitly recorded selected (a,x) pairs at
p53. These include zero x, x=a, nearby codes, exponent-field boundaries,
and the known p48 witness. The witness fails at p48 and passes at p53,
providing a nontrivial negative control for the exact rounding helper.
The ten brackets, five nonsquares, 40 q16 odd-A parity combinations,
and 24 other parity combinations were also checked exactly.

These 143 pairs do not establish universal p53 sufficiency. Universal
coverage in this audit comes from the independently reviewed mathematical
classification and complete case exclusions, not from the selected pairs.
No proof assistant was used. This is an independent construction within
the same language-model/team workflow, not independent human replication.

9. Recommended presentation clarification

Make the next threshold's left-hand binade explicit in the crossing lemma.
The sentence giving midpoint distance 2^(E-p) is valid on E_S because
N/D>2^24 makes E+S>=24 and the next threshold cannot skip a binade
boundary. For much smaller ratios in a more general domain, a separate
separation argument is needed before using r's exponent for that distance.
This is a self-containment improvement to the reviewed S53 argument,
not an identified S53 counterexample or a missing case in its proof.

The proof should continue to label p53 as conservative. Nothing here
closes sharp p49 sufficiency, the complete normal-scale review, formal
verification, closest-theory derivability, or novelty.
